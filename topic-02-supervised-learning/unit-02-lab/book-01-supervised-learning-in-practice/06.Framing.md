## Exercise 6.1: Walk the chain

Run this chain out loud for the default problem:

- **Decision:** who acts on the prediction, and what do they do? (A lender decides whether to extend credit, tighten a limit, or intervene early.)
- **Target:** given that, `default` (yes/no) is the honest target, and a *probability* of default is more useful to the lender than a bare label.
- **Metric:** a missed defaulter is expensive, so recall matters; but false alarms annoy good customers and cost business, so you can't ignore precision. Accuracy is useless here, as you've now shown.
- **Threshold:** set by the relative cost of those two errors, not by 0.5.

**Task:** Not one of those steps mentioned the algorithm. Swap logistic for the histgb model and every decision stays the same. Write two sentences arguing why the framing, not the model choice, is the hard and valuable part.
