# Week 4 Lab: Safety, Governance and Explainability

## Objectives

By the end of this 90-minute lab, you'll be able to:

- Explain a model globally with feature importance and SHAP, and locally with LIME, and read the plots honestly
- Say where each explanation stops being trustworthy, and back that up with a proxy and a stability check on real numbers
- Stress a model with a deliberate distribution shift and measure the failure it hides rather than the one it announces
- Situate a model in GDPR and the EU AI Act risk tiers, and say precisely what that placement obliges you to do
- Author a model card and a datasheet that disaggregate the numbers and admit their own limits
- Judge whether an explanation has earned trust or merely performed it

## Prerequisites

- The Week 2 lab, where you built this default classifier, and the Week 3 lab, where you audited it for fairness across `SEX`
- Python 3.9+ with `numpy`, `pandas`, `scikit-learn` and `matplotlib`, plus `shap` and `lime` (install with `pip install shap lime`)
- The data file `UCI_Credit_Card.csv`, provided with this lab. It is the same file as Weeks 1 to 3, and it is in **Week 04 Lab Files** on this unit if you need it again

> **Dataset and provenance.** We use the **UCI "Default of Credit Card Clients"** dataset: Yeh, I. and Lien, C. (2009), *UCI Machine Learning Repository*, [https://doi.org/10.24432/C55S3H](https://doi.org/10.24432/C55S3H), licensed **CC BY 4.0**. It records 30,000 credit-card clients of a Taiwanese bank over April–September 2005. This is the *same* dataset you have used since Week 1, and the model you interrogate today is the *same* logistic classifier you built in Week 2 and audited in Week 3. Carrying one model across the term is deliberate: by now you know it well enough to spend your attention on the safety, governance and explanation questions rather than on the modelling. It is also available via the `ucimlrepo` package (`fetch_ucirepo(id=350)`) and on Kaggle: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/uciml/default-of-credit-card-clients-dataset

> **One model, all the way through.** Everything in this lab, including the Section 7 exercises, uses this credit dataset and this one model. There is no project model or second dataset to bring. Going deep on a model you already know is the point.
