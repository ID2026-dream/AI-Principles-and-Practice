# Section 5: Govern it, GDPR, the EU AI Act, and a governance note

## What You'll Learn

Governance answers one question: when this model harms someone, who is responsible, and how do they show they were careful? This section situates your model against the two instruments that bind it, and turns that into a note someone could actually be held to.

## Exercise 5.1: Situate the model

**Task (no code):** Place your default classifier against each instrument, and write one specific sentence for each rather than a slogan.

- **GDPR, Article 22.** The model makes an automated decision with a significant effect on a named person's access to credit. If it is deployed with no human in the loop, which right does that trigger, and what would putting a human "meaningfully" in the loop have to look like to change the analysis?
- **EU AI Act.** Creditworthiness assessment is named in Annex III, so this system is **high-risk**. That word is a to-do list carried *before* the model decides about anyone: a risk-management system, data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, human oversight, demonstrated accuracy and robustness, and transparency to those affected. Which two of those obligations does today's lab already produce evidence for?
- **NIST AI RMF.** The four functions (govern, map, measure, manage) run as a loop, not a gate. Which function does your Section 4 monitoring plan belong to?

**Question:** GDPR tells you to collect *less* data (minimisation) and the EU AI Act tells you to document *more*. Name one concrete place those two pull against each other for this model, and how you would satisfy both.

## Exercise 5.2: Draft the governance note

A governance note is one page. A box-tick note asserts; a strong note shows its working. Draft the strong version for this model, and make every line specific to what you measured today.

**Task (writing, no code):** Write a note with these five lines filled in from your own results:

- **Tier and obligations:** the EU AI Act tier, and the two obligations you are actually addressing.
- **Explanation, and its limit:** what SHAP and LIME said drives the model (`PAY_1`, and `SEX` as a top-three driver), and the one sentence on why that is not a fairness defence.
- **Robustness:** the downturn result, stated as the frozen approval rate against the rising bad-loan rate.
- **Monitoring:** the leading indicator from Exercise 4.2 that you would watch in production.
- **Refusal:** the one thing you would refuse to deploy without, for example automated denial with no human review.

The grade, in the assignment, sits in those specifics: which tier, which obligation, which failure mode, which refusal.
