## What You'll Learn

Oversight needs something to oversee. A model card is the label on the tin, and its honesty lives in the sections people would rather leave blank: the disaggregated metrics and the caveats. You'll compute the numbers those sections need, then write the card around them.

## Exercise 6.1: The numbers the card cannot dodge

A single accuracy figure is a slogan. The card needs performance broken out by group, which is exactly where the Week 3 fairness audit reappears inside this week's paperwork.

**Step 1:** Disaggregate the model's behaviour across `SEX`:

```python
# section6_disaggregate.py
from sklearn.metrics import recall_score
sex = Xc_te["SEX"].values
print(f"{'group':8s} {'n':>5s} {'decline rate':>13s} {'recall':>8s} {'base default':>13s}")
for s, label in [(1, "male"), (2, "female")]:
    m = (sex == s)
    print(f"{label:8s} {m.sum():5d} {pred[m].mean():13.3f} "
          f"{recall_score(y_true[m], pred[m]):8.3f} {y_true[m].mean():13.3f}")
```

You'll see the model decline **8.4%** of men against **6.4%** of women, and catch defaulters at different rates too (recall about **0.249** for men, **0.230** for women), on top of different base default rates (**0.244** vs **0.206**). One overall accuracy number hid all of that. These are the lines a strong model card puts front and centre, not in a footnote.

## Exercise 6.2: Author the model card

**Task (writing):** Fill in a model card for this classifier. The easy sections are honest one-liners; the last two are where the marks are.

- **Intended use:** rank applicants by default probability for a human loan officer to review.
- **Out-of-scope use:** automatic denial with no human; any population it was not trained on.
- **Metrics:** overall accuracy 0.812; but disaggregated, the decline and recall numbers you just computed across `SEX`.
- **Ethical considerations:** the documented disparate impact from the Week 3 audit, and the plain fact that `SEX` is a top-three driver.
- **Caveats:** the approved book degrades under a downturn (Section 4); do not trust the model past its monitoring window without re-validation.

**Reflection (datasheet, no code):** A model card describes the model; a datasheet describes the data underneath it. Answer three datasheet questions for this dataset: who is in it and who is missing (it is one Taiwanese bank's *approved* clients in 2005, so who never appears?); how was the `default` label defined and by whom; and what use would be inappropriate (would you deploy a 2005 Taiwanese model on 2025 Irish applicants?). Most model failures are dataset failures wearing a model's clothes, and this is where you catch them.
