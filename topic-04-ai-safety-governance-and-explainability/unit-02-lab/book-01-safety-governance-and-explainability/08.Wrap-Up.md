## Key Takeaways

1. **A safety failure is the model working as specified and harming anyway.** Your tests check the cases you thought of; the harm lives in the ones you did not.
2. **Global and local explanations answer different questions.** Importance and SHAP describe the model across everyone; LIME describes one decision. Do not quote one to answer the other.
3. **A faithful explanation is not an exoneration.** SHAP honestly showing that `SEX` drives the model is a description of an unfair model, not a defence of it.
4. **The frozen metric is the dangerous one.** Under the downturn the approval rate did not move while the book rotted; monitor the leading indicators you *can* see, and design that monitoring before deployment.
5. **Documentation's honesty lives in the sections people leave blank.** Disaggregated metrics and stated caveats are the whole point; "none identified" means nobody looked.

## Troubleshooting

**`ModuleNotFoundError: shap` or `lime`?**
- Install them into your environment: `pip install shap lime`. They are the only libraries beyond the Week 2 base stack.

**A SHAP message about subsampling the background?**
- Harmless. It is telling you it used a sample of the training rows as the reference distribution, which is exactly what we asked for.

**Different numbers from the ones quoted?**
- Small differences are fine; make sure `random_state=0` is set on the split, the models, and the SHAP background sample. The permutation and LIME steps use sampling, so expect the last decimal to wobble.

**Everything is slow?**
- Permutation importance and the LIME calls are the heavy steps. `n_jobs=-1` uses all cores for permutation importance, and you can drop `num_samples` in LIME to 2000 while experimenting.
