## Key Takeaways

1. **Unsupervised learning proposes structure; it does not discover truth.** With no label there is no scoreboard, so the output is a hypothesis you must defend, not a fact you found.
2. **Choosing K, or where to cut, is a modelling decision with your name on it.** The elbow and silhouette inform the argument; here they roughly agreed on a weak, lopsided `K=2`, and you still had to interpret what it meant.
3. **PCA learns a representation, and you must read its axes.** `PC1` here is a bill-size axis, and naming it is what turned a scatter plot into an interpretation.
4. **Strong structure can be irrelevant structure.** The clearest split in the data separated big spenders, and defaulted at the same rate as everyone else. Real does not mean useful.
5. **Recoverability is a property of the data, not a law.** `SEX` was barely recoverable here (`0.612` against a `0.604` base rate) and strongly recoverable in the lecture (`0.71`). "We dropped the column" is a claim about your schema; only the probe tells you whether it is a claim about your geometry.

## Troubleshooting

**`(30000, 25)` not printing?**
- Check `UCI_Credit_Card.csv` is in your working directory. If you fetched it via `ucimlrepo`, make sure the target column is named `default.payment.next.month` before you rename it.

**The `KMeans` scan is slow?**
- Seven fits with `n_init=10` on 30,000 rows takes a little while. Drop `n_init` to 5 while experimenting, or scan a shorter range of `K`. Keep `random_state=42` so your numbers match.

**Different numbers from the ones quoted?**
- Small wobbles are fine; the silhouette uses a random `sample_size`, so its last digit moves. Make sure `random_state=42` is set on `KMeans`, the silhouette call and the PCA projection, and `random_state=0` on the recovery split.

**`ConvergenceWarning` from the Gaussian mixture or logistic regression?**
- Harmless here. The mixture message means it hit the iteration cap on messy real data; the recovery probe is only being used to read off an accuracy, not to be tuned.
