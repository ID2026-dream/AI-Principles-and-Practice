# Week 10 Lab: Inside the Transformer

> **Before the lab starts: download the models.** This lab loads `distilbert-base-uncased` and `gpt2`, a one-time
> download of about 750 MB. On a shared lab network that download alone can swallow a quarter of your session, so do
> it **before** the clock starts: unzip the lab files and run `python precache_models.py` once, at home.
>
> With the weights cached, the whole lab runs in three or four minutes of compute; the 45 minutes is thinking and
> writing. If you cannot pre-cache, do Sections 1 and 2 first so the downloads finish while you read.

Last week you **adapted** a pretrained model. This week you stop training altogether and **interrogate** one somebody
else trained. Today you build almost nothing: you take a finished model, freeze it, and open it up. "A good result"
today usually means "a number I can defend", not "a number that went up".

By the end of this 45-minute lab you will be able to:

- extract and visualise attention from a real pretrained transformer, name what one head is doing, and test that claim
  on a second input;
- run an in-context-learning demonstration with the weights frozen, and read a small model's modest result as the
  lecture's scaling claim made concrete;
- probe a model's hidden states layer by layer against an honest baseline, and say exactly what a probe does and does
  not license (LO4);
- measure the quadratic cost of attention yourself and name a failure mode that an accuracy number hides.

## The data

**AG News** topic classification: Zhang, X., Zhao, J. and LeCun, Y. (2015), *Character-level Convolutional Networks
for Text Classification*, NIPS 28, [arXiv:1509.01626](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01626). The four largest classes,
**World, Sports, Business and Sci/Tech**, with 30,000 training and 1,900 test items each (120,000 train, 7,600 test).
The corpus is provided *for non-commercial research purposes*: treat it as teaching and research only.

Four balanced classes, so **your baseline to beat is 0.25**, not fifty per cent.
