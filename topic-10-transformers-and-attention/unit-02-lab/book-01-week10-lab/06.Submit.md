# Submitting This Lab

**This lab is graded.** Your submission is **one file**: `SUBMISSION.md`, with your recorded numbers and your
answers in it.

## 1. Finish the sheet

Every question from **1a** to **6** should have something under it. Add your name and student number at the top.
Record **your own** numbers: the model-dependent ones (attention maps, probe accuracies, the in-context curve) depend
on the checkpoint and the sample, so they will differ from anyone else's, and that is expected.

## 2. Upload it

**[Upload your Week 10 submission here](UPLOAD-LINK-TO-ADD)**, by the deadline shown on Moodle.

Name it `week10-<student number>-SUBMISSION.md`. Just that file.

## 3. How it is marked

The plots are the easy part. The marks are in the interpretation (lecture slide 43, booklet Section 16.6):

| A box-tick answer | A strong answer |
|---|---|
| "Here is an attention heatmap." | Names what a specific head does, shows it holds on a second input, and breaks where it should. |
| "Accuracy went up with more shots." | Reads the in-context curve, with its spread, as evidence about where the ability lives, and says what it does not prove. |
| "The probe scored 0.8 at layer 6." | Argues what "linearly decodable at layer 6" means, and what it does not, against the baseline. |
| Prints the plots, and ends there. | States one claim the evidence cannot support, and why. |

The reference answers are released after the deadline.
