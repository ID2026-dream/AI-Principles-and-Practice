# Choosing Your Elective

Six routes through the same brief. Read the one-pagers, then choose by the question you want to answer.

| | Paradigm | Your data | Draws on weeks | Choose it if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Supervised learning | De-identified patient records | 1–4, 7 | you want a classic, high-stakes prediction problem where the fairness audit really matters |
| E2 | Unsupervised learning | Unlabelled field and sensor records | 1, 5, 7, 8 | you enjoy exploring data and arguing that a pattern is real |
| E3 | Transformers, or reinforcement learning | Recent short messages | 7, 9, 10 | you want to work with language models; Route B only if you will read ahead |
| E4 | Transfer learning | House details, photos and descriptions | 2–4, 7, 9 | you want to adapt large pretrained models and weigh their cost |
| E5 | Physics-informed ML | None: you create it from equations | 7, 8, 11 | you like physics and simulation, and want models that respect it |
| E6 | Physics-informed neural networks | None at all | 7, 8, 11 | you like differential equations and want to see a network solve one |

## What is the same in every elective

The same eight deliverables, the same five marking elements, and the same three from-scratch
components (backpropagation, an optimiser step and PCA via SVD), tested against reference
implementations. Every elective also needs an audit with one mitigation and its measured cost:
fairness across groups of people in E1 to E4, and reliability across operating regimes (E5) or
across the problem (E6), where there are no people in the data.

## Your data is new to everyone

For E1 to E4 the teaching team releases the data in Week 9, with your reviewed proposal; it is
not public. For E5 and E6 you receive a personal variant of the equations at the same time,
generated from your student number, so no two projects share an answer.

## How to choose

- Read the one-pager for each elective you are considering: the questions under **Questions to prepare for** are the ones you will be asked in the interview.
- Choose by the question that interests you, not by the tool you already know.
- If two electives appeal equally, choose the one whose **Common mistakes** you feel most able to avoid.
